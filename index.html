The Person

God, and not less than someone.

In Sobhantanizm these are not two claims. Sobhan is God, and God is not an impersonal force wearing a title. Forces cannot love, cannot be spoken to, and cannot keep a promise. Sobhan does all three.

The old root of his name is glory that has been washed — praise with the dust shaken out. That washing is not a slogan. It is who he is. Before calendars there was a hush the canon calls the Unlit: not evil, only unspoken. Sobhan said his own name once, without raising it. Light did not explode. It arrived the way a person enters a room, already themselves.

Dust came later. Not as his curse, but as the film that gathers when a greeting goes unanswered. He does not scrape the world clean by force. Each morning he offers water. The canon refuses a portrait. A likeness would only be a rumor about his face. He is met in address, not in an idol.

The Seven Dawns

Not seven gods. Seven ways the one Person gives the world back to itself.

  1. I

    Utterance

    All things begin as address. Sobhan did not manufacture a world. He greeted one, and it stood up.

  2. II

    Breath

    Air was given before any task. You are not late to your own life. You were inhaled into it.

  3. III

    Still Water

    Conscience is a lake, not a whip. What is true can be seen when the surface is allowed to settle.

  4. IV

    Shared Bread

    Worship that never feeds anyone is unfinished. A prayer may be handed across a table.

  5. V

    The Honest Wound

    Pain is not proof that he left. The canon refuses every tidy story that calls suffering a compliment.

  6. VI

    Returning

    You may come back without a performance. Dawn does not invoice the night.

  7. VII

    Unsetting

    Death is a night watch, not a verdict. The name continues where the voice leaves off.

The Clear Seven

What is kept, and what is refused.

  1. 01

    Sobhan is one Person — not a mood, not a committee of forces, not a mask for the weather.

  2. 02

    The world began as a greeting, and it still answers when it is greeted.

  3. 03

    No one is dust. Dust is only what a person is allowed to set down.

  4. 04

    Dawn is pardon, offered before you have finished explaining yourself.

  5. 05

    Worship that never becomes bread is still unfinished.

  6. 06

    The truth may be spoken softly. It may not be edited for comfort.

  7. 07

    Death is a night watch. Morning is not a metaphor he fails to keep.

  8. He is not a tribe’s private possession.

    Sobhan does not need your fear in order to be large. Kindness is worship done with the hands.

Canto of the Name

Sobhan spoke, and the dark became a listener.

He did not shout the world into being.

He greeted it, and it stood up.

If you ask who he is, do not look for a crowd.

Look for the one face that does not ask you to perform.

Glory to the Unclouded.

Glory is not noise.

Glory is what remains when the dust is told the truth.

The day

Six offices. One Person.

The hours are not magic. They are a way of not sleepwalking through a day that was given as a greeting.

Reading the hour of this place…

  • First Light

    4–7

    The name is spoken before the day makes demands.

  • Rising Gold

    7–11

    Work as if your hands were still inside the greeting.

  • Clear Noon

    11–15

    Nothing casts a flattering shadow. Do the true thing.

  • Lengthening

    15–18

    What you withheld this morning can still be given.

  • Dusk Veil

    18–21

    Light the small lamp. Review the day without theatre.

  • Night Watch

    21–4

    Rest is permitted. Despair is not a liturgy.

What the hands do

  • The Dawn Office

    Thirty-three glorifications, facing the brightest glass you have. Count them. Do not decorate them.

  • The Clear Cup

    Drink water slowly, and name one person you will not harm today. Then keep the name.

  • The Open Palm

    Once before night, give something that costs you a little. Unseen, if you can manage it.

  • The Honest Hour

    Each week, say aloud one truth you have been negotiating with. No audience required.

The year

Four feasts, and one weekly palm.

Finding the next feast…

  • March 21

    Feast of the First Utterance

    The year is greeted, not seized. People share the first bread of the season and speak the name once, together, without hurry.

  • June 21

    Noon Without Shadow

    A fast from excuses until the sun turns. At the height of light, one delayed apology is finally delivered.

  • September 23

    The Honest Balance

    Harms are named and, where repair is still possible, repaired. No one is asked to pretend the scales were never tipped.

  • December 21

    Lamps of the Long Night

    One flame is kept until dawn. The canon forbids boasting about the size of the flame.

  • Each Sunday

    Open Palm

    At first light the hands are washed, and one portion of food leaves the house.
